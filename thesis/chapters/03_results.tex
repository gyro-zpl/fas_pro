\section{Individual-Level Results}

The main in-distribution evaluation uses the respondent protocol restricted to
the 2016, 2018, and 2019 waves: 2,726 rating rows from the 20\% hash holdout,
whose respondent numbers do not appear in \texttt{train\_r}. Baselines are HistGB, a
gradient-boosting model \citep{friedman2001gbm}, and Ridge trained on
\texttt{train\_r} with the same features, plus the base model with the same
prompts but no fine-tuning. Confidence intervals are bootstrap 95\% intervals
based on the nonparametric bootstrap \citep{efron1993bootstrap}, over rows for
MAE and correlation and over respondent--year groups for choice.

Table~\ref{tab:pas} reports the result. The fine-tuned LLM reaches an MAE of 21.35
[20.87, 21.83], a correlation of 0.666 [0.652, 0.680], and a choice accuracy of
0.525 $\pm$ 0.033 across seeds (range 0.490--0.569; tie-aware scoring gives
0.481) against chance 0.163. HistGB reaches 20.73 [20.16, 21.26], 0.703 [0.684,
0.724], and 0.543; Ridge is weaker at 22.86, 0.639, and 0.450. A
second checkpoint, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B, trained under the same recipe
reaches 21.37, 0.665, and 0.572 (Appendix~\ref{app:second}); the in-distribution
signal is therefore not specific to Qwen3-8B.

The honest reading is that the LLM is competitive with, but not superior to, a
strong tabular baseline. A paired bootstrap over the same rows gives an
LLM--GBM MAE difference of $+0.59$ $[-0.17,+1.32]$ for seed 42, $+0.42$
$[-0.34,+1.19]$ for seed 43, and $+0.89$ $[+0.10,+1.67]$ for seed 44; the
difference is small and only the seed-44 interval excludes zero, with the
baseline never worse. The argmax choice difference is mixed ($+0.026$
$[-0.005,+0.057]$, $-0.028$ $[-0.067,+0.010]$, and $-0.052$
$[-0.103,-0.002]$), while tie-aware choice consistently favors the baseline
($-0.057$ $[-0.092,-0.023]$, $-0.082$ $[-0.122,-0.040]$, and $-0.083$
$[-0.134,-0.036]$). The correlation difference is consistently negative
($-0.040$, $-0.033$, and $-0.039$). A respondent-level cluster bootstrap on
seed 42 widens the MAE interval from $[-0.17,+1.32]$ to $[-0.40,+1.61]$ and
keeps the correlation interval negative ($[-0.062,-0.019]$); the conclusions are
unchanged. Fine-tuning, by contrast, improves MAE by 2.5--3.0 relative to the
base model (seed 42: $-2.85$ $[-3.74,-2.03]$; seed 43: $-3.01$
$[-3.95,-2.08]$; seed 44: $-2.54$ $[-3.50,-1.64]$), with a correlation gain of
about $+0.07$ and a choice gain of $+0.01$ to $+0.09$ depending on seed. The
language model therefore adds a unified interface and a rating distribution, not
superior point prediction.

Predictions are correlated with the truth but compressed toward the middle of the
scale: on the in-distribution rows the LLM's prediction standard deviation is
about 40, versus 24 for HistGB. The per-year breakdown and the 2024 collapse are
reported in Chapter~\ref{ch:temporal}.

\section{Population-Level Results}

For each GSS configuration (EDC and EHC), the 2024 joint distribution is
reconstructed from eight historical waves, and 400 synthetic respondents are
sampled and compared with the empirical 2024 sample (1,601 for EDC, 1,552 for
EHC). JS divergence is reported on the three marginals and the three pairwise
joints; lower is better. As a reference, we also draw 400 rows directly from the
reconstructed joint $Q^{\ast}$ without a language model.

\input{tables/table3_fas}

Direct sampling from $Q^{\ast}$ reaches a marginal JS of 0.0004 [0.0002, 0.0023] and
a joint JS of 0.0076 [0.0070, 0.0141] on EDC; on EHC it reaches 0.0011 [0.0006,
0.0036] and 0.0225 [0.0192, 0.0308]. These are lower than every LLM condition on
both metrics. The prior is therefore the strongest distributional baseline in
this task. The result is stable across sampling seeds: over repeated 400-row
draws, EDC marginal/joint JS ranges 0.0004--0.0012 / 0.0064--0.0076 and EHC
0.0011--0.0028 / 0.0190--0.0225.

The LLM results should be read against that baseline. On EDC, the base model
produces 0.0552/0.1181; direct SFT reduces these to 0.0048/0.0160 (91\%/86\%), and
v3 reaches 0.0040/0.0215 (93\%/82\%). On EHC, the base model starts at
0.0319/0.1001; direct SFT reaches 0.0167/0.0470 (48\%/53\%), and v3 reaches
0.0077/0.0363 (76\%/64\%). Fine-tuning therefore moves the LLM substantially
toward $Q^{\ast}$, but it does not surpass $Q^{\ast}$: the prior remains better on both
marginal and joint JS in both configurations. EDC is the stronger configuration
for the LLM; EHC is weaker, and the large-reduction claim is scoped to the
LLM-versus-base comparison.

Bootstrap 95\% CIs over the generated or prior-sampled rows separate the base
model from v3 on both configurations (EDC marginal 0.0552 [0.0476, 0.0644] vs 0.0040 [0.0022,
0.0077]; EHC joint 0.1001 [0.0874, 0.1188] vs 0.0363 [0.0332, 0.0458]) and
separate the prior from v3 on the joint metric (EDC 0.0076 [0.0070, 0.0141] vs
0.0215 [0.0181, 0.0305]; EHC 0.0225 [0.0192, 0.0308] vs 0.0363 [0.0332,
0.0458]). The SFT and v3 intervals overlap, so the two SFT runs are not ranked.
The full table is in Appendix~\ref{app:fas}.

Component analysis shows that RAG alone is close to the base model (EDC 0.0473,
EHC 0.0404) and that RAG plus SFT performs like direct SFT. The movement
toward $Q^{\ast}$ comes from supervised exposure to the reconstructed distribution
and the five task types, not from retrieval. Quota annealing (EDC 0.0091, EHC
0.0175) is worse than direct SFT; the quota run did not converge within 15
rounds, so the comparison is descriptive.

The base model returns 351 of 400 rows on EHC (coverage 0.877), while the SFT runs
return 399--400. Charging every missing row as maximally wrong raises the base
model's EHC marginal/joint JS from 0.0319/0.1001 to 0.1168/0.1850, while the
fully covered v3 run stays at 0.0077/0.0363; on EDC, direct SFT's 0.0048/0.0160
becomes 0.0377/0.0489, whereas v3 is unaffected at 0.0040/0.0215. The v3 run is
therefore the cleanest LLM result. On EHC, direct SFT's joint symmetric KL
increases from 0.4289 to 0.5487 even though JS falls, so we do not use it as the
EHC headline; v3 improves both JS and KL (0.0363 and 0.3064 vs 0.1001 and
0.4289), which is why v3 is the EHC reference run.
